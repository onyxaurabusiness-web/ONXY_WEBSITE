I

Chapter I · The Place

It begins high in the Himalaya.

Our springs rise between 2,000 and 3,000 metres: above the Kullu Valley in Himachal Pradesh, in the Garhwal Himalaya of Uttarakhand, and in the high valleys of the north-west. Granite and gneiss; pine, fir and alpine grass below the snowline, bare rock above it. Winter buries the slopes in snow, and the melt feeds the springs through the year. At first light the peaks catch the sun long before the valleys do.

II

Chapter II · The Journey

Time did most of the work.

Snow and glacier melt sink into fractured rock and move slowly underground before they surface as a spring. In the western Himalaya, isotope studies have measured snowmelt supplying between 55 and 96 percent of the flow of mountain springs. The rock filters the water on the way, and lends it its minerals.

  • 3 millionsprings in the Indian Himalayan Region, out of some five million across India.NITI Aayog, 2018
  • 50 millionpeople in the twelve Himalayan states rely on those springs.NITI Aayog, 2018
  • 55–96%of spring flow in the western Himalaya comes from snowmelt; glacier melt and rain make up the rest.Jeelani et al., Hydrogeology Journal

III

Chapter III · The Keeper

Someone is always watching over it.

Each source is looked after every day by our source partner’s team on the mountain. They walk up to the intake at first light, check the flow and the screens, draw the day’s samples and log the readings. The water runs by gravity from the spring into a closed tank, and it is never left standing open to the air.

Chapter IV · The Proof

Measured before it is trusted.

Every batch is measured before it is bottled: pH every four hours on the line, TDS for every batch, and a full panel, microbiology and heavy metals included, every month at an accredited laboratory. The report for each batch is available on request.
